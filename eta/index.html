MsouWout — what is actually working

Checked on 13 September 2026 against the live system, not from memory.

How each line was checked, and what I did not check. You asked for three things before anything counts as done: the backend works, the app is connected to it, and the whole workflow has been run for real.
  • The first two I checked myself — every route the server defines, matched against every call the app really makes, plus the browser tests listed at the bottom.
  • The third one I cannot do alone. It needs a real passenger and a real driver. Where I have not seen a real run, I say so instead of calling it finished.
One honest note on method: matching a server route to the app that calls it is harder than it sounds, because the app builds its web addresses in pieces. My first automated pass reported the emergency button as disconnected. It is not — it is wired correctly. I checked each line below by hand for that reason.

Does not exist and must not be claimed

WhatStatus
SMS, WhatsApp or push messageNothing, anywhere. Every notice only appears while the person has the page open. This is the one you hit with “M rive”.
Paying for a ride in the appHalf. The payment endpoints are live on the server. No screen in either app calls them. Every ride is cash.
Zone filteringNot connected. The zone system exists on the server; no screen uses it. A driver in Cap-Haïtien can still be offered a Delmas ride if he is the nearest one online.
Passenger account & trip historyDoes not exist. There is no passenger account by design — name and phone are taken inside the order.
RatingsDoes not exist.
Driver PIN at loginNot enforced. Anyone with a driver's number can open that driver's dashboard. Recorded in July, still true.
The pattern behind almost every surprise on this project The server half was built and works; the app half never calls it. That was true of the vehicle filter, the partner dashboard, GPS position, the passenger's map, the emergency button, and payments. When something “should already work”, this is nearly always why — and it is the first thing I now check.

Needs your decision

WhatWhy it is yours, not mine
The map service behind every pricePrices come from the free public OpenStreetMap lookup. It rate-limits — it blocked my machine for over 20 minutes during testing. When it is blocked it returns nothing, and the fare falls back to a guess. A paid map service, or prices set by zone, removes that. Both cost money, so it is your call.
NotificationsReal SMS or WhatsApp costs money per message and needs an account in your name.
Payments in the appFinishing this means real money moving in a test. I will not run that without you watching and without agreeing the amount first.
