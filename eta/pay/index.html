What is genuinely missing

Seven things. The first three are the launch blockers.

GapWhat it means
Nothing asks for money The blocker. The passenger app never calls the payment endpoints. Every ride today is cash by default.
A paid ride would not know it was paid Missing. The payment code records nothing against the ride. Even if the app called it, the ride stays marked unpaid and the driver would still ask for cash.
Coming back after paying Missing. The gateway takes the passenger to its own page to pay. Nothing brings them back or notices they paid.
The gateway never calls us back Design constraint. There is no automatic notification — the only way to learn a payment succeeded is to ask. So the server has to check on its own, or a passenger who closes the app pays and the ride never updates.
MsouWout writes to no ledger Missing. Rides live in one database, the ledger in another, and nothing connects them. This is your "one HaitiBiznis ledger".
Refunds do not move money Missing. Plus the refund admin screen is pointed at a different ride database from the one real rides are stored in — so a real MsouWout ride could not be matched to a refund request today.
Driver payouts Does not exist. Nothing pays a driver out. Today the driver keeps the cash, which is why this has not bitten yet.
One thing I cannot answer for you: cards. You asked for cards. The payment code accepts MonCash, NatCash and Kashpaw. I can see no card option in it, and I cannot log in to your SolutionIP account to check what your merchant profile has switched on. Please ask SolutionIP whether your account can take cards, and on which method name. If it can, it is a small addition. If it cannot, cards need a second provider and that is a separate job — I am not going to promise you cards before I know.

The stages, and what each costs

Priced separately, smallest useful piece first. You pay for each one as it lands, and you can stop after any of them. Stage 1 alone is a working, launchable product.

Stage 1 — A passenger can pay for a ride Needed for Monday$200

The only stage you need in order to launch.

  • The passenger chooses MonCash, NatCash or cash when ordering, in all four languages the app already speaks.
  • The app opens the gateway's payment page and brings them back.
  • The ride records how it was paid, the amount, the reference and the time.
  • The server checks the payment itself on a timer, so a passenger who closes the app or loses signal still ends up with a ride marked paid.
  • The driver's screen says PAID or COLLECT CASH — so he never asks a paying passenger for money again.
  • You get a screen listing payments, with the gateway's reference against each ride.
  • Written tests, and a real end-to-end run with you watching.
Stage 2 — One HaitiBiznis ledger After launch$120

What you asked for: the money recorded centrally, with a reference back to the ride that produced it.

  • Every MsouWout payment also writes into the HaitiBiznis ledger.
  • MsouWout keeps its own ride data — only the money is centralised.
  • One place to see takings across rides, orders and tickets together.
Stage 3 — Refunds that actually return money $150

Separated out, as you asked, because it is the one part that needs something from the provider.

  • Point the existing refund workflow at the real ride database.
  • Approving a refund sends the money back and reaches "processed".
  • The ledger entry flips to refunded.

Depends on SolutionIP. I need to know whether their API can reverse a payment. If it cannot, the honest version is a recorded manual transfer — still worth building, but say so now rather than discover it later.

Stage 4 — Paying drivers out $160

Only becomes urgent once passengers pay in the app, because then the money reaches you first instead of the driver.

  • What each driver is owed, after your commission.
  • Pay out on a schedule, recorded in the same ledger.
  • A statement the driver can see, so he can check it himself.

These are four separate prices, not a total and not a package. If you only ever do Stage 1, you owe $200 and you have a product that takes money.
