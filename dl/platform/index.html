Your eight questions

Straight answers

1. Can a merchant register, log in, create a store, add products, receive an order, confirm it and complete the sale with no manual help?
YES for pickup — delivery needs one more thing

I walked the whole chain tonight against a real copy of the backend. Register → log in → store created → product added → customer orders → merchant confirms, prepares, marks ready → delivered → the shop is credited. No one touches it by hand.

The one gap: no delivery driver can sign up on MyPlopPlop. The driver form on myplopplop.com sends applications to the MsouWout system instead, so the list the delivery engine looks at is empty and always has been. Tell me how you want that handled and I will wire it.

2. Is the LajanMaker referral permanently attached to every merchant, driver and customer, so commissions are never lost?
NO — and this is the biggest gap on the platform

This is the honest picture, and it is not good:

  • Merchant — only since last night, and only as a name carried on the link (?ref=SPAP). It is written once and cannot be changed later, which is right. But it is a name, not a linked LajanMaker account.
  • Driver — the form does ask "who referred you", but it is a fixed list: FTPH, COTRASMOTHA, SPAP, Eglise Shalom, Facebook, WhatsApp, friend/family, other. An individual LajanMaker agent cannot be chosen at all, and the answer is stored in the MsouWout database, not MyPlopPlop.
  • Customer — nothing. No referral is recorded when a customer signs up, ever.

There is a full commission system already built in the backend — agent codes, 10% on MyPlopPlop and MsouWout, tiers, payouts, the lot. It has a working way to record a referral. No sign-up page has ever called it, so not one referral has been recorded through it.

And it is not permanent even in the design: a recorded referral expires 365 days after sign-up.

I have not changed any of this tonight, because "permanently attached" is a decision about your business, not a bug I can guess at. Two things I need from you: should an agent's credit last for ever or for a year? And should the customer sign-up also carry the agent's code? Once you answer, I will connect all three sign-ups to the commission system.

3. Is every payment tied to a valid order before money is accepted? I never want money collected without an order.
YES — it was not, twice, and both are fixed

Last night: a shopper with no account could reach Place Order, be sent to MonCash with an order number the page invented, pay, and leave no order anywhere. Fixed — no account means no order and no payment, and if the order fails to save we refuse to charge.

Tonight I found the other half. After paying, the page checked with the gateway in the browser and then just drew the success screen. It never told the server. So every sale you have ever taken is still marked unpaid: the shop was never credited when the order was delivered, and no order was ever eligible for payout. Now the server asks the gateway itself, checks the amount matches the order, and only then marks it paid. The browser is not allowed to say "it is paid".

4. Are merchant, customer and driver registration and checkout all writing to the database with no false success messages?
YES for three of the four
  • Merchant registration — was the "sham" you found. Rebuilt last night, 23 checks including every way it can fail.
  • Customer registration — writes correctly, always has.
  • Checkout — fixed, see question 3.
  • Driver registration — writes correctly, but into the MsouWout database. On MyPlopPlop there is no delivery-driver sign-up at all. Nothing lies to anyone; the page simply is not there.
5. How does a completed order get to a delivery driver today? What exactly happens between "Ready" and "Delivered"?
It works now — it never did before tonight

What happens, with nobody dispatching by hand:

  1. The shop marks the order Ready.
  2. A delivery job is created automatically and the customer gets a 4-digit handover code.
  3. It is offered to the nearest driver who is approved, online and within 15 km.
  4. He has 30 seconds. If he declines or does not answer, it moves to the next closest driver by itself, and keeps trying.
  5. He accepts, collects, and the order shows his name and number.
  6. At the door the customer gives him the 4-digit code. Without it he cannot finish the delivery.
  7. Order closes, the shop is credited, the driver is paid.

Every step of that was walked end to end tonight, including trying the wrong handover code and being refused.

Why it never worked before: the dispatch crashed on every single order because of one bad line, and the crash was written to a log nobody reads. The order still said "Ready", so nothing looked wrong. On top of that, no shop has GPS — so a 6 km delivery was measured as 8,148 km and priced at 203,808 HTG, and the search for a driver "within 15 km of the shop" was looking in the Atlantic Ocean with a rider standing 6 km away. Both fixed.

Still needed: at least one approved delivery driver, which is question 1.

6. Is there an admin screen where I can approve or reject merchants, drivers and businesses?
YES — and I was wrong last night

Last night I told you there was no way to approve a driver. That was wrong: I had only looked at your console. The admin screens exist and they work — drivers with approve and refuse buttons, merchants, orders, riders, settings, at myplopplop.com/admin/.

What was actually missing was a way for you to get in. Those screens need an account marked "admin", and until tonight the only way to get one was a hole that let anyone on the internet have it (see the bugs below).

Do this once, after the deploy.

Open your ecosystem console → the security panel → "Bay yon moun dwa administratè" → type your own number → press the green button. Then log in on myplopplop.com with that number and the admin pages open. The panel also lists everyone who holds it.

7. Does every important action generate a log, so we can audit registrations, orders, payments, deliveries, cancellations and commissions?
Money and orders: yes. A single audit log: no.

What is recorded today: every record carries the date it was created and last changed. Every order keeps its own times — paid, confirmed, prepared, picked up, delivered, cancelled — plus who cancelled it and why. Every movement of money is its own row: payment, driver earning, commission, referral bonus, each tied to the order number. Driver approvals and refusals now record who did it and when (refusals did not, until tonight).

What is not: there is no one place you can open and read "what happened on the platform today". Nothing records who changed a price or edited a shop. The only proper audit trail in the system covers the savings groups, nothing else.

A real audit log is a build, not a fix, so I have not started it. Say the word and I will send you a plan for it.

Bugs

🐞 Found in the sweep — all fixed, all free

These are real, and every one of them was silent. None of them made anything look broken on screen, which is why they survived so long.

  • Anyone on the internet could make themselves an admin. The sign-up form copied whatever role you asked for onto the new account, and "admin" was allowed. That gave a stranger every user's name and phone number, every order, every transaction, and the power to approve drivers. I proved it on a copy, never on your live system. Closed: the sign-up form now only allows customer, merchant and driver, and admin is granted from your console.
  • Every sale stayed unpaid. The success screen came from the browser; the server was never told. So no shop was ever credited and nothing could ever be paid out. Fixed.
  • The delivery dispatch crashed on every single order since it was written — one field the database refused. The error went to a log and the order still said "Ready", so nothing looked wrong. Fixed. The same bug also broke the ride-request endpoint.
  • Deliveries were measured from the middle of the Atlantic. A 6 km delivery came out as 8,148 km and 203,808 HTG, and no driver was ever found because the search was looking at sea. Fixed.
  • A finished delivery never closed its order. The goods were in the customer's hands while the order still said "Ready", the tracking page never completed, and the shop was never credited. Fixed — one shared ending for both the pickup and the delivery case.
  • Refusing a driver left no trace of who refused him. Approving recorded it; refusing did not. Fixed.
  • From last night: buying without an account took the money and created no order; the order recorded delivery as free when the customer had paid for it, so the rider's share came out of nothing. Both fixed.
